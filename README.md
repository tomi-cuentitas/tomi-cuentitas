## About

I am a doctoral researcher in theoretical and computational quantum information at the Instituto de Física La Plata (CONICET–UNLP). My research focuses on developing efficient methods for the classical simulation of quantum many-body systems, particularly state-adapted approximations and Heisenberg-picture dynamics.

My work originated in maximum-entropy inference and generalized mean-field projections. More recently, I have developed a covariance-based approach to **state-adapted Pauli propagation** in collaboration with Gabriela Wójtowicz and Martin B. Plenio.

From January to November 2025, I worked as a *Wissenschaftlicher Mitarbeiter* at the Institut für Theoretische Physik, Universität Ulm. Our research collaboration has continued beyond the end of that appointment. In September 2026, I will join the CAI-3 group at Los Alamos National Laboratory as a Graduate Research Assistant, working with Marco Cerezo.

## Featured research

### State-adapted Pauli propagation

**[State-adapted generalized mean-field projections for Pauli propagation of Heisenberg dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12840)**  
F. T. B. Pérez, G. Wójtowicz, and M. B. Plenio · *arXiv:2609.12840 (2026)*  
[Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12840) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.12840)

Conventional Pauli-weight truncation discards operator contributions above a prescribed weight. Our approach instead introduces a **state-adapted covariance inner product** and an associated **orthogonal generalized mean-field projection** that maps many-body operators onto statistically relevant low-body representatives.

Combined with adaptive projected Krylov dynamics in the Heisenberg picture, this construction improves accuracy relative to conventional weight truncation in the reported benchmarks at comparable computational cost. The numerical demonstrations include three-dimensional lattices of up to **90 spins**, with retained Pauli weight up to **m = 3**. The work also examines representation and dynamical errors and their connections to operator entanglement and, under additional assumptions, nonstabilizerness.
